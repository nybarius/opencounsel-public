# Pain-point map

Real pains of litigation practice, each matched to what OpenCounsel does, the mechanism behind it,
the evidence we can show today, one sentence of buyer language, and the public source for the
pain. No number appears here that is not in a cited source; where a figure is not sourced, it is
left out.

| Pain | Our capability | Mechanism | Evidence | Buyer language | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sanctions for AI-hallucinated citations | Every authority is identity-checked against the uploaded public opinion and its exact quotation; an unverifiable cite is refused, not softened | Deterministic source verification; a refusal carries its witness | Pass 2 of the demo (`docs/AUTHORITY_PIPELINE.md`); `test_cite_check.py` | "No citation leaves this office that the system could not put its hand on." | *Mata v. Avianca, Inc.*, No. 22-cv-1461 (PKC) (S.D.N.Y. June 22, 2023) (sanctions order); *Park v. Kim*, 91 F.4th 610 (2d Cir. 2024) |
| Lawyers' duties when using generative AI: competence, confidentiality, supervision, fees | Local-first pipeline that sends no client prose to a hosted model; every automated step is a supervised, receipted rule | Recorded rules with receipts; the privilege gate; disabled network and model policies | `docs/PUBLISH_READINESS.md`; `scripts/privilege_audit.py`; `src/opencounsel/contracts/models.py` | "Supervision is a table you can read, not a promise." | ABA Standing Comm. on Ethics & Prof'l Responsibility, Formal Opinion 512, *Generative Artificial Intelligence Tools* (July 29, 2024) |
| Court standing orders requiring AI-use disclosure or certification | The package states exactly which passes ran, on which inputs, with which reads; the certification is generated from the receipts, not written from memory | Receipts name every consumed read by digest | The final review ZIP's records (`docs/BRIEF_PIPELINE.md`) | "The certification writes itself from the audit trail." | Judge Brantley Starr (N.D. Tex.), *Mandatory Certification Regarding Generative Artificial Intelligence* (May 30, 2023); Fifth Circuit proposed Rule 32.3 (Nov. 2023, not adopted June 2024) |
| E-filing rejections for formatting, pagination and linking defects | Pass 1 applies the court profile: typography, heading hierarchy, linked TOC and categorized TOA, Roman front matter then Arabic body page 1 | Deterministic profile application; the original stays immutable | Pass 1 of the demo; `docs/FRONT_MATTER.md`, `docs/WORD_STYLES.md` | "The clerk's checklist runs before you click file." | S.D.N.Y., *Electronic Case Filing Rules & Instructions*; the court profiles in `src/opencounsel/templates/profile_data/` |
| Cite-checking and table-of-authorities hours | The TOA and cite-check are compiled, not typed; corrections are ledgered items for lawyer review | Correction ledger with review status per item | `test_toa_categories.py`, `test_cite_check.py` | "Hours of cite-checking become minutes of review." | *The Bluebook: A Uniform System of Citation* (21st ed. 2020) (the standard the compiler applies) |
| Confidentiality risk of sending client matter to cloud AI | Nothing leaves the machine; the network and model policies are disabled by contract | `network_policy = disabled`, `model_policy = disabled` in the service contract; loopback-only UI | `src/opencounsel/contracts/models.py`; `docs/WEB_INTERFACE.md` | "Your matter never leaves the building." | ABA Formal Opinion 512, Part II.B (confidentiality); ABA Model Rule 1.6 |
| Showing a defensible audit trail of how a filing was produced | Every artifact carries its hash, its inputs and the reads consumed; the trail replays | Receipts, hashes, immutable originals | The final review ZIP: correction, publication, acquisition, source-verification, link and hash records | "Rule 11's reasonable inquiry, on paper, for every filing." | Fed. R. Civ. P. 11(b) |
| Clients and courts asking how a decision was reached | "Why did it decide that": the decision's class and the fact that decided it, in plain English, the same every time | The decision's class read from the ledger and rendered as one fixed sentence; the same class always gives the same sentence | The UI panel; `opencounsel.explain` | "Every automated decision explains itself in one sentence you can hand to the client." | ABA Formal Opinion 512, Part II.D (communication); Starr certification order (above) |
| Transparency and adverse-action-reason duties | The same panel gives the specific reasons for an adverse determination, from the table, not a model | The same fixed sentences over the correction ledger's classes | `opencounsel.explain` | "Specific reasons, from the record, on demand." | Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (AI Act), arts. 13 and 50; Regulation B, 12 C.F.R. 1002.9; CFPB Circular 2022-03 (May 26, 2022) |
