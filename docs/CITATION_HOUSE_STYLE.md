# Citation house style

OpenCounsel separates three questions that ordinary citation software often conflates:

1. whether an authority and citation are correct;
2. whether quoted text and its omission or alteration disclosures are accurate; and
3. how a reviewed citation should be expressed in this practice's house style.

The first two are verification questions. The third is presentation. A style preference must never
erase a disclosure that the source comparison shows is substantively required.

## Baseline and software limits

The Bluebook remains the baseline citation grammar for United States litigation unless a court,
judge, local rule, or jurisdiction-specific convention controls. It is not treated as a complete
practice-writing style guide.

Zotero uses Citation Style Language for ordinary citation and bibliography rendering, but Zotero's
own documentation describes its United States legal-citation support as limited and recommends
Juris-M for sustained legal work. Database citations and many legal-source distinctions are not
fully supported. CSL is useful as a declarative formatting model, not as an authority-verification,
quotation-lineage, short-form-resolution, or proposition-support engine.

OpenCounsel therefore does not delegate legal citations to Zotero or CSL. It may reuse CSL ideas at
the presentation boundary, but source identity, quotation fidelity, quotation ancestry, signals,
short forms, and characterization remain separate typed records.

## Omission parentheticals

The preferred forms are concise:

- `citation omitted`
- `citations omitted`
- `quotation omitted`
- `quotations omitted`
- `quotation and citation omitted`
- `quotation and citations omitted`

Do not add `internal` or `external` merely to describe the location of omitted material. Thus:

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| `internal citation omitted` | `citation omitted` |
| `external citations omitted` | `citations omitted` |
| `internal quotation marks omitted` | `quotation omitted` |
| `internal quotation marks and citations omitted` | `quotation and citations omitted` |

This is a review rule, not an automatic substitution rule. The verifier must first determine what was
actually removed. For example, a citation-only omission cannot be rewritten as a quotation omission,
and a nested quotation cannot lose a required disclosure simply because the shorter wording is
preferred.

## Other quotation disclosures

Retain the shortest accurate form among:

- `alteration in original` / `alterations in original`;
- `emphasis added` / `emphasis omitted`;
- `brackets in original` when that fact matters;
- `cleaned up` only when the governing court and filing context permit it and the underlying
  transformations have been verified.

OpenCounsel records each transformation separately. A rendered parenthetical is generated only after
that record is reviewed.

## Case citations and TOA entries

A body citation may contain a signal, pincite, subsequent history, explanatory parenthetical, and
quotation disclosure. A canonical TOA entry does not inherit all of that surrounding prose. It uses
the reviewed long-form authority identity, italicizes only the case name, and aggregates page
references from full and resolved short citations.

## Relationship to finished briefs

The current house-style evidence shows a consistent division:

- major document sections and principal argument points are all caps;
- subordinate factual and argument headings are title case;
- omission parentheticals favor concise forms such as `citation omitted`, `citations omitted`, and
  `quotation and citation omitted`;
- the word `internal` is not part of the preferred omission vocabulary.

These choices are encoded as an office overlay. They do not modify court-rule profiles or silently
rewrite matter text.
